05 / DEFINITIONS AND NUMERICAL CHECKS

What is held fixed, and what the numbers mean

The waveform and dynamics

  • Cutoff-subtracted root-Lorentzian envelope multiplied by [1 − (2t/L)²]⁵. The unsigned midpoint value is one; there is no pulse-area or signed-peak renormalization.
  • A 16 ns sine sign change at the midpoint. Corrected DRAG is Q = −β(dI/dt)/(2π|α|), with β = −0.5 and complex drive I − iQ. The derivative includes both the envelope and the sign transition.
  • Three-level Duffing-transmon Lindblad dynamics; α = −216 MHz, T₁ = 51.24 µs, T₂ = 7.31 µs. No added phenomenological Stark correction.
  • 100 drive scales from 0 to 100 MHz, 201 detunings from −0.5 to +0.5 MHz, and a maximum time step of 0.25 ns, refined locally around the pulse core.

FWHM and ideal SNR

  • FWHM is the nearest half-depth width after subtracting a linear background fitted at |detuning| ≥0.4 MHz. The center search is restricted to ±0.25 MHz.
  • Widths spanning fewer than six detuning intervals are rejected. Additional minima with population prominence ≥0.005 are counted; accepted does not necessarily mean a single isolated dip.
  • 500 shots at the center and 250 at each ±0.45 MHz shoulder. Signal = (pₗ + pᵣ)/2 − p꜀; variance = [2p꜀(1−p꜀) + pₗ(1−pₗ) + pᵣ(1−pᵣ)]/1000.
  • SNR excludes readout assignment error, technical noise, drift, and the cost of locating the center. It is a simulation estimate, not measured hardware SNR.

Duration changes the core too. With this cutoff parameterization, σ = (L/2)c/√(1−c²). A fixed cutoff and a longer pulse therefore change the central width as well as the tails. At cutoff 1, the normalized envelope uses the continuous limit [1−(2t/L)²]⁶.

__CONVERGENCE_SUMMARY__

Reference linewidth and convergence scope

The default width is FWHM / Γ₂ = πT₂ × FWHM (in Hz), with Γ₂ = 1/(πT₂) ≈43.5 kHz for T₂ = 7.31 µs. A value of 1 means one T₂ reference linewidth; dashed width lines show this reference. The selector switches all width charts, tables, and the overview image to kHz. SNR/FWHM ratios keep their labeled MHz⁻¹ units, and archived fwhm_hz arrays remain in Hz. This normalization does not claim that the shaped pulse reaches the weak-drive coherence limit. This study uses a fixed finite detuning window; inferred widths can depend on the chosen background. Representative finer-grid results are saved under validation/convergence.json. They do not establish convergence of every point or every possible optimum.
