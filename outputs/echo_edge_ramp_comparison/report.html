Can smooth endpoints improve echo spectroscopy?

Goal: a narrow resonance dip with good visibility across high drive amplitudes. Each cutoff fixes the underlying Lorentzian width. Only the endpoint window changes within each group.

At 100 MHz and base cutoff 0.001, the polynomial window gives 174.3 kHz FWHM versus 302.3 kHz for hard endpoints. Its contrast is 0.394 versus 0.231; ideal SNR is 13.6 versus 7.5.

Matched physical settings

20 µs duration; 16 ns sine echo transition; β = −0.5; α = −216 MHz; T₁ = 51.24 µs; T₂ = 7.31 µs. Nominal amplitude 0–100 MHz, 100 points; detuning −500 to +500 kHz, 201 points. Lindblad RK4, dt = 0.5 ns. Ramps occupy the first and last 1, 3 or 6 µs of the existing 20 µs pulse.

SNR is an ideal projection-noise estimate, not experimental SNR. 1000 total shots: 500 at the dip center, 250 at each of ±450 kHz. Signal is the mean shoulder Pe minus the center Pe. Noise includes independent Bernoulli variance at those three points. Perfect Pe classification is assumed; technical noise, drift and center-search overhead are absent.

Separate shaping from loss of pulse area

Each curve is plotted against A × (windowed unsigned I-area / hard unsigned I-area). Equal horizontal coordinates therefore mean equal unsigned I-area within a given cutoff. This is a re-expression of the simulated grid, with lines joining samples. Compare only the overlapping horizontal range; the windowed pulse requires a higher nominal amplitude for the same area.

What changed in the waveform?

Quintic ramps use w(u) = 10u³ − 15u⁴ + 6u⁵, with u from zero at the endpoint to one at the ramp join. The polynomial uses w(t) = [1 − (2t/L)²]³. Both have zero value, slope and curvature at the outer endpoints. DRAG is recomputed from the complete windowed I envelope: Q = −β I′/(2π|α|). No amplitude or area normalization is applied in the primary comparison.

Interpretation and reproducibility

FWHM uses the nearest half-depth crossings after a linear background fit at |detuning| ≥ 400 kHz. Center is the raw central minimum refined by three-point quadratic interpolation. No Gaussian fit or smoothing. Contrast/SNR uses the actual ±450 kHz shoulders. Width and contrast depend on this measurement window.

The high-drive range is defined here as 50–100 MHz. A narrow feature with negligible contrast is not useful. Final Pf is not maximum transient leakage. The three-level model omits higher transmon states, transfer-function distortion and readout errors. Results concern this finite pulse and these coherence parameters.

Numerical checks

Checked 4 window/core combinations at three high amplitudes. Maximum population difference: 8.5e-05; maximum FWHM change from dt = 0.5 to 0.25 ns: 9.0 Hz; from 5 to 2.5 kHz detuning steps: 54.6 Hz. Full numerical and equal-area checks.
