Conversely, the Lorentzian echo variant demonstrates an amplitude-independent linewidth spanning over three orders of magnitude in drive strength, evidencing remarkable robustness against amplitude-induced dephasing. This insensitivity arises from the adiabatic evolution and the symmetric frequency-splitting intrinsic to the pulse design. The primary limitation lies in the floating offset and amplitude-dependent baseline, resulting in reduced contrast compared to conventional square and Lorentzian drives.



To identify the useful operating regime, we examine the linewidth and contrast
of the echo response as functions of pulse duration and drive amplitude.  We
choose pulse durations shorter than $T_1$, but long enough that Fourier
broadening does not dominate.  The duration-dependent linewidth, contrast,
and contrast-weighted resolution are reported in the Supplemental
Material~\cite{SupplementalMaterial}.

A second design constraint is the cutoff of the formally infinite-support
pulse envelope.  Truncation introduces a residual endpoint drive
$\Omega_c=c\Omega_0$ and a corresponding cutoff-broadening
contribution~\cite{boradjiev_qubit_2013,mihov_defying_2024,mihov_qubit_2024}.
The relevant weak-saturation condition is
$s_c=\Omega_c^2T_1T_2\ll1$, or
$\Omega_c\ll1/\sqrt{T_1T_2}$; the inequality therefore points toward
\emph{smaller}, not larger, endpoint amplitudes.  The relative cutoff $c$
cannot be specified independently of $\Omega_0$, $T_1$, and $T_2$, giving a
three-dimensional design space of pulse duration, drive amplitude, and cutoff
level.

The waveform itself offers a further control of the linewidth--contrast
tradeoff. A derivative removal by adiabatic gate (DRAG) pulse adds a
quadrature component proportional to the time derivative of the in-phase
waveform to suppress leakage and drive-induced phase
errors~\cite{motzoi_drag_2009,gambetta_analytic_2011}.
Figure~\ref{fig:pulse-improvement} compares completed measurements
for a $20~\mu\mathrm{s}$ echo-root-Lorentzian pulse with $c=0.005$: an uncorrected outer
envelope, the same envelope with DRAG, and DRAG with a polynomial edge
correction. All three use the same sine-smoothed phase jump $s(t)$ of
duration $T_s=50~\mathrm{ns}$ at the midpoint.
The corrected pulse is
\begin{align}
    L_{\mathrm{floor}}^{(1/2)}(t)
    &=\frac{L^{(1/2)}(t)-c}{1-c},\\
    W_p(t)&=\left[1-\left(\frac{2t}{L}\right)^2\right]^p,
    \qquad p=2,\\
    s(t)&=\begin{cases}
        +1, & t\leq-T_s/2,\\
        -\sin(\pi t/T_s), & |t|<T_s/2,\\
        -1, & t\geq T_s/2,
    \end{cases}\\
    L^{\mathrm{smooth}}(t)
    &=L_{\mathrm{floor}}^{(1/2)}(t)\,s(t)\,W_p(t).
\end{align}
Here $|t|<L/2$, $\tau=(L/2)/\sqrt{c^{-2}-1}$, and $W_p=0$ outside
the pulse. The floor correction removes the endpoint offset, and
$W_p(t)$ smooths the edges. Thus, (a)--(b) isolate DRAG,
while (b)--(c) isolate this edge correction.

\begin{samepage}
With $I(t)=A L^{\mathrm{smooth}}(t)$, the added experimental DRAG
quadrature is
\begin{equation}
    Q(t)=-\frac{\beta_{\mathrm{acq}}}{\alpha}\frac{dI(t)}{dt},
    \label{eq:drag-quadrature}
\end{equation}
\end{samepage}
where $\alpha=\omega_{12}-\omega_{01}<0$ is the signed qubit anharmonicity.
We use this DRAG convention throughout the main text and Supplemental
Material. The experimental DRAG coefficient was fixed at
$\beta_{\mathrm{acq}}=1.25$ for this comparison, which uses
$T_s=50~\mathrm{ns}$ and $p=2$.
The ideal phase-compensation value $\beta=0.5$ can differ from experimental
optima because of pulse distortions; our fixed-$\beta_{\mathrm{acq}}$
comparison does not determine an experimental optimum.
Resonance-center stability does not establish minimum leakage, since
phase-error and leakage suppression can favor different DRAG
coefficients~\cite{chen_leakage_2016}.
At a Rabi drive of $50~\mathrm{MHz}$, the supplemental simulations without
DRAG ($\beta=0$) give a maximum final leakage of about $4.4\%$ for an
abrupt inversion ($T=0$), reduced to $0.035\%$ by a $16~\mathrm{ns}$
transition~\cite{SupplementalMaterial}.
Each variant is scaled separately to cover the same calibrated
complex-envelope peak $\Omega_{\mathrm{pk}}=\max_t\sqrt{I(t)^2+Q(t)^2}$
from 0 to $100~\mathrm{MHz}$ in cyclic-frequency units. Figure~3 and the
statistics below use the $0$--$\LatestThreeDisplayMaxRabi~\mathrm{MHz}$ window.
This peak-amplitude convention differs
from the nominal pre-envelope amplitude $\Omega_0$ used in Fig.~2
and in the retained numerical scans.

Pulse shaping progressively stabilizes the resonance center
[Fig.~\ref{fig:pulse-improvement}(d)]. On common accepted amplitudes, the
RMS variation about each curve's mean falls from approximately
$\LatestEchoCenterRms~\mathrm{kHz}$ for the echo to
$\LatestDragCenterRms~\mathrm{kHz}$ with DRAG and
$\LatestPolyCenterRms~\mathrm{kHz}$ with the added polynomial correction.
This measures amplitude-dependent center motion, including fit noise,
rather than absolute frequency accuracy.
With DRAG alone, the median $\mathrm{FWHM}/\Gamma_{T_2^*}$ is
$\LatestDragWidthNormalized$, while
the polynomial correction roughly halves it to
$\LatestPolyWidthNormalized$. Median dip contrast also increases from
about \LatestEchoContrast{} for the echo to \LatestDragContrast{} with DRAG
and \LatestPolyContrast{} with polynomial shaping.
Contrast is the fitted dip depth relative to a local linear background,
in measured excited-state fraction; no readout-fidelity correction is applied.
The corrected median linewidth remains approximately \LatestPolyLimitRatio{}
times $\Gamma_{T_2^*}$. This dataset therefore demonstrates improved measured
line shape and center stability, without establishing a coherence-limited
linewidth, a sensitivity gain, or a global waveform optimum.

The polynomial-corrected DRAG echo retains a narrow response at strong drive,
potentially extending to still higher Rabi amplitudes. At approximately
$60~\mathrm{MHz}$, its $\mathrm{FWHM}/\Gamma_{T_2^*}$ is about $4.4$
[Fig.~\ref{fig:pulse-improvement}(f)], compared with roughly
$7\times10^3$ from the two-level steady-state square-pulse estimate---about
three orders of magnitude broader. Higher-level excitation can further degrade
the square-pulse spectrum beyond this idealized estimate.

Figure~\ref{fig:duration-cutoff-scan} maps the joint dependence on cutoff
and drive amplitude in a numerical study of a
$20~\mu\mathrm{s}$ polynomial-corrected DRAG echo, with $p=2$, a
$16~\mathrm{ns}$ midpoint transition, and $\beta=0.5$.
The 100 drive samples are logarithmically spaced
from 1 to $100~\mathrm{MHz}$. This survey uses nominal drive scales
and retains the earlier relaxation parameters and midpoint transition,
rather than the $50~\mathrm{ns}$ transition and actual peak normalization
of the measured Fig.~3.
The smoothed white contours indicate the high-contrast band near $C=0.2$.
At sampled points with fitted central-dip depth $C>0.2$, the simulated response
combines appreciable contrast
with fitted $\mathrm{FWHM}/\Gamma_{T_2^*}$ values of approximately $2.9$--$8.4$.
The contours select contrast; the linewidth within that region is read
independently from panel (a).

The useful band shifts toward smaller cutoff as drive increases, yet a
single cutoff can retain both narrow linewidth and high contrast over a
wide drive range. For example, at the sampled
$c=1.048\times10^{-3}$, the fitted $\mathrm{FWHM}/\Gamma_{T_2^*}$ remains below $3.51$
and contrast above $0.296$ throughout the sampled $10.235$--$100~\mathrm{MHz}$
interval. Here $c$ sets the original root-Lorentzian core width even though
the corrected envelope vanishes at its endpoints: changing $c$ also
changes $\tau$. The full duration survey and numerical checks are given
in the Supplemental Material~\cite{SupplementalMaterial}.
