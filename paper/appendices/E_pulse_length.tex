\section{Pulse-Duration Dependence}
\label{sec:pulse-length}

A finite interrogation time introduces Fourier broadening, while decoherence
sets the long-duration floor.  For a square pulse, the central spectral lobe
narrows approximately as $1/L$ at short duration.  Once $L$ is comparable to
the transverse coherence time, extending the pulse yields progressively less
improvement and the linewidth approaches the Bloch-equation scale in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:t2-limited-angular-linewidth}.  The precise crossover depends on
the pulse envelope and on the linewidth estimator; it is therefore obtained
from the complete simulated response rather than from an order-unity rule.

\begin{figure}
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/length_scan.png}
    \caption{Numerical square-pulse linewidth, normalized to the coherence
    scale $T_2^{-1}$, as a function of pulse duration.  Short pulses follow
    the Fourier-limited trend, whereas long pulses approach a
    decoherence-limited plateau.}
    \label{fig:square-pulse-length-scan}
\end{figure}

For the echo-root-Lorentzian sequence, changing $L$ can also alter the
endpoint amplitude unless $\tau$ and $c$ are controlled independently.
Relaxation over long pulses reduces contrast and changes the off-resonant
background even when the central feature continues to narrow.  Consequently,
the useful duration is chosen by considering linewidth and contrast together.

\begin{figure}
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/spectroscopy_length_comparison.png}
    \caption{Numerical echo-root-Lorentzian response for the pulse durations
    indicated in the panels.  Increasing the duration initially narrows the
    resonance; for durations comparable to the relaxation and coherence
    times, dissipation changes the baseline and reduces the usable contrast.}
    \label{fig:echo-pulse-length-comparison}
\end{figure}
