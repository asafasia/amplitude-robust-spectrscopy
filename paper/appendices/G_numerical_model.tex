\section{Numerical Model}
\label{sec:numerical-model}

The unitary trajectories in main-text Fig.~1 solve the two-level
Schr\"odinger equation with QuTiP's state-vector solver
\cite{johansson_qutip2_2013}, recording populations at 1600 output times.
They omit relaxation and dephasing; these output times are not the
solver's internal integration steps.

The dissipative simulations use either two or three levels, as specified
for each comparison below, with Markovian relaxation and dephasing.
Main-text Figs.~2--4 use the three-state transmon model.
In the frame rotating at the drive frequency, and within the
rotating-wave approximation, the real-drive Hamiltonian is
implemented as~\cite{PhysRevA.76.042319}
\begin{equation}
    \frac{H(t)}{\hbar}
    =
    -\Delta a^\dagger a
    + \frac{\alpha}{2}a^{\dagger 2}a^2
    + \frac{\Omega_0}{2} f(t)
      \left(a+a^\dagger\right),
    \label{eq:numerical-hamiltonian}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta=\omega_d-\omega_{01}$ is the drive--qubit detuning, $\Omega_0$ is
the peak Rabi frequency, $\alpha$ is the anharmonicity, and $a$ is the
truncated oscillator lowering operator.  In the effective two-level model,
$a=|0\rangle\langle1|$ and the anharmonicity term vanishes.
In the two-level case, up to an irrelevant identity term,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:numerical-hamiltonian} is equivalent to
$H/\hbar=(\Delta/2)\sigma_z+(\Omega_0 f/2)\sigma_x$.

The unmodified pulses follow
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:finite-generalized-lorentzian}--\eqref{eq:echo-pulse-definition};
the smoothed envelopes and DRAG quadrature are defined in
Sec.~\ref{sec:pulse-modifications}.
The overall sign of the echo envelope is a phase convention and does not
affect the measured populations.

The density matrix evolves according to the Lindblad master equation
\begin{equation}
    \dot{\rho}
    =
    -\frac{i}{\hbar}[H(t),\rho]
    + \mathcal{D}\!\left[\sqrt{\frac{1}{T_1}}\,a\right]\rho
    + \mathcal{D}\!\left[\sqrt{\frac{2}{T_\phi}}\,
      a^\dagger a\right]\rho,
    \label{eq:numerical-master-equation}
\end{equation}
where
$\mathcal{D}[C]\rho=C\rho C^\dagger-\frac{1}{2}
\{C^\dagger C,\rho\}$ and
\begin{equation}
    \frac{1}{T_2}=\frac{1}{2T_1}+\frac{1}{T_\phi}.
\end{equation}
The primary-qubit calculations use
$T_1=\MeasuredTOne~\mu\mathrm{s}$ and
$T_{2,\mathrm{eff}}=\EffectiveTTwo~\mu\mathrm{s}$, corresponding to
$T_\phi=\EffectiveTPhi~\mu\mathrm{s}$.
The primary-qubit anharmonicity is inferred from repeated OPX1000 two-photon
spectroscopy.  With $f_{01}=4.267106667~\mathrm{GHz}$, three scans locate the
sharp, interior response at $f_{02}/2=4.159106667~\mathrm{GHz}$, including a
$1~\mathrm{MHz}$-step confirmation scan.  We therefore use
$\alpha/(2\pi)=2(f_{02}/2-f_{01})=\MeasuredAnharmonicity~\mathrm{MHz}$; the
finest scan gives a grid-scale uncertainty of approximately
$1.0~\mathrm{MHz}$ for the inferred anharmonicity.
Each calculation starts in $|0\rangle$, integrates
Eq.~\eqref{eq:numerical-master-equation} over the complete pulse, and records
the final state observables. Spectroscopy maps repeat this evolution over
the detuning and drive grids specified for each comparison, using the
corresponding pulse duration, cutoff, and coherence parameters.

Main-text Fig.~2 uses fourth-order Runge--Kutta integration of the
three-level density matrix, with 12\,800 uniform time steps in each
$10~\mu\mathrm{s}$ half of the $20~\mu\mathrm{s}$ pulse
($\Delta t=0.78125~\mathrm{ns}$). The two halves are integrated separately
so that no step crosses the abrupt sign reversal. Each simulated map has
126 drive values selected from the broad experimental grid and 401
detunings over either $\pm25$ or $\pm0.5~\mathrm{MHz}$.
Doubling the steps per half at representative drive--detuning points
changes $P_e$ by at most $1.61\times10^{-5}$ for the ordinary pulse and
$4.62\times10^{-6}$ for the echo. Figure~\ref{fig:simulation-results}
uses the same integrator at all 501 acquired detunings and the five
selected experimental drive values, with its convergence check described
in Sec.~\ref{sec:fixed-amplitude-spectra}.
These calculations retain the states
$|g\rangle$, $|e\rangle$, and $|f\rangle$, with
$\alpha/(2\pi)=\MeasuredAnharmonicity~\mathrm{MHz}$, and report
$P_e=\rho_{ee}$ while retaining
$P_f=\rho_{ff}$ in the archived figure data.  The pulse-shaping calculations in main-text Figs.~3 and 4 use the same
three-level Lindblad structure with complex drive $I-iQ$, including the
derivative of the complete signed envelope. Figure~3 uses a $0.5~\mathrm{ns}$
time step; Fig.~4 uses a maximum $0.25~\mathrm{ns}$ step with a grid adapted
to the pulse core. The latter scan covers five durations, 100 logarithmic
cutoffs from $10^{-5}$ to 1, 100 drive amplitudes from 0 to $100~\mathrm{MHz}$,
and 201 detunings over $\pm0.5~\mathrm{MHz}$. Main-text Fig.~4 shows selected
slices of this scan. Widths spanning fewer than six detuning intervals,
unresolved dips, and nonpositive contrast are rejected. Representative
checks at three cutoffs and three amplitudes for each duration halve the
maximum time step and double the detuning intervals; among mutually accepted
traces, the largest FWHM changes are $0.255~\mathrm{kHz}$ and
$0.117~\mathrm{kHz}$, respectively. This is a sampled convergence check,
not an error bound for every scan point. The three-level calculations in
Sec.~\ref{sec:pulse-modifications} likewise use fourth-order Runge--Kutta
integration, with the time steps and refinement checks given alongside
each comparison. The dense pulse-length calculation uses the sampled
waveform and campaign-specific parameters described in
Sec.~\ref{sec:dense-pulse-length}.

The duration--resolution comparison in
Fig.~\ref{fig:duration-resolution-comparison} instead integrates the
two-level Bloch equations with fourth-order Runge--Kutta steps in
$u=\operatorname{arsinh}(t/\sigma)$, where
$\sigma=(L/2)/\sqrt{c^{-2}-1}$. The transformation
$t=\sigma\sinh u$ gives $dt/du=\sigma\cosh u$ and concentrates physical-time
samples near the pulse core. Each half uses 1600 uniform steps in $u$,
with the sign reversal imposed between halves. The spectra are evaluated
at 100 logarithmically spaced drives from $0.001$ to $50~\mathrm{MHz}$
and 201 detunings over $\pm0.5~\mathrm{MHz}$; the displayed drive range
ends at $25~\mathrm{MHz}$. These calculations capture
coherent drive dynamics, relaxation, and pure dephasing, but do not describe
leakage, higher-level AC-Stark shifts, or multiphoton transitions.  The
main-text Figs.~2--4, fixed-amplitude spectra in
Sec.~\ref{sec:fixed-amplitude-spectra}, dense pulse-length comparison in
Sec.~\ref{sec:dense-pulse-length}, and high-drive analyses in
Secs.~\ref{sec:pulse-modifications}--\ref{sec:extended-cutoff-duration} use the
explicit three-level model.
