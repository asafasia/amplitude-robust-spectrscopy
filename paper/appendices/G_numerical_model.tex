\section{Numerical Model}
\label{sec:numerical-model}

The unitary trajectories in main-text Fig.~1 use a two-level
Schr\"odinger model without relaxation or dephasing, solved with
QuTiP~\cite{johansson_qutip2_2013}. Dissipative spectroscopy simulations
use two or three levels, as specified for each comparison.
In the drive's rotating frame, within the rotating-wave approximation,
the real-drive Hamiltonian is~\cite{PhysRevA.76.042319}
\begin{equation}
    \frac{H(t)}{\hbar}
    = -\Delta a^\dagger a
    + \frac{\alpha}{2}a^{\dagger 2}a^2
    + \frac{\Omega_0 f(t)}{2}(a+a^\dagger),
    \label{eq:numerical-hamiltonian}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta=\omega_d-\omega_{01}$, $a$ is the truncated oscillator
lowering operator, and $\alpha/(2\pi)=\MeasuredAnharmonicity~\mathrm{MHz}$
is the measured transmon anharmonicity. The drive scale $\Omega_0$ and
pulse envelope $f(t)$ follow
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:finite-generalized-lorentzian}--\eqref{eq:echo-pulse-definition}.
For two levels, $a=|0\rangle\langle1|$ and the anharmonicity term vanishes.
Smoothed envelopes and the complex drive $I-iQ$ used with DRAG are defined
in Sec.~\ref{sec:pulse-modifications}.

Relaxation and pure dephasing enter through
\begin{equation}
    \dot{\rho}
    = -\frac{i}{\hbar}[H(t),\rho]
    + \mathcal{D}\!\left[\sqrt{\frac{1}{T_1}}\,a\right]\rho
    + \mathcal{D}\!\left[\sqrt{\frac{2}{T_\phi}}\,
      a^\dagger a\right]\rho,
    \label{eq:numerical-master-equation}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{D}[C]\rho=C\rho C^\dagger-
\{C^\dagger C,\rho\}/2$ and
$T_2^{-1}=(2T_1)^{-1}+T_\phi^{-1}$.
Each calculation starts in $|0\rangle$ and evolves over the complete pulse
using the coherence parameters documented for its dataset.
Three-level calculations retain $|g\rangle$, $|e\rangle$, and $|f\rangle$;
the displayed observable is $P_e=\rho_{ee}$, with $P_f$ retained in the
archived data.
