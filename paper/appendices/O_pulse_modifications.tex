\section{Pulse Modifications: Phase Inversion, DRAG, and Cutoff-Edge Smoothing}
\label{sec:pulse-modifications}

Midpoint smoothing suppresses leakage, DRAG compensates drive-induced
phase errors, and cutoff-edge shaping improves linewidth and contrast
at strong drive. The comparisons below use $L=20~\mu\mathrm{s}$,
$c=0.005$, and $\alpha/(2\pi)=-216~\mathrm{MHz}$.

\subsection{Smoothing the midpoint phase jump}

We replace the abrupt sign reversal by
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
    s_T(t)=
    \begin{cases}
        +1, & t<-T/2,\\
        -\sin(\pi t/T), & |t|\leq T/2,\\
        -1, & t>T/2,
    \end{cases}
    \\
    I(t)=\Omega_0 g(t)s_T(t),
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:pulse-modification-sign}
\end{equation}
where $g(t)$ is the unsigned even envelope and $T$ is the transition
duration. This real-drive interpolation passes through zero amplitude.
Its odd symmetry preserves zero signed area and hence the ideal resonant
two-level cancellation described in Sec.~\ref{sec:pulse-definition}.

At $\Omega_0/(2\pi)=50~\mathrm{MHz}$ and without DRAG ($\beta=0$),
increasing $T$ from 0 to $16~\mathrm{ns}$ reduces the largest final $P_f$
over the displayed detunings from $4.44\times10^{-2}$ to
$3.48\times10^{-4}$, about a factor of 128
[Fig.~\ref{fig:midpoint-leakage}]. This measures residual population
in $|f\rangle$ after the pulse. The DRAG and cutoff comparisons below
use a $50~\mathrm{ns}$ transition.

\subsection{DRAG and resonance stability}

For the complex-drive convention $I-iQ$, DRAG acts on the complete
signed waveform~\cite{motzoi_drag_2009,gambetta_analytic_2011}:
\begin{equation}
    Q(t)=-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\frac{dI}{dt}
    =-\frac{\beta\Omega_0}{\alpha}
    \left[g'(t)s_T(t)+g(t)s'_T(t)\right],
    \label{eq:pulse-modification-drag}
\end{equation}
with signed anharmonicity $\alpha=\omega_{12}-\omega_{01}<0$ in
angular-frequency units, as in the main text. The second term accounts for
the midpoint inversion; a finite $T$ keeps its derivative finite.
The leading-order phase-compensation solution for a transmon
\cite{gambetta_analytic_2011} gives
\begin{equation}
    Q_{\mathrm{phase}}(t)\simeq-\frac{1}{2\alpha}\frac{dI}{dt}
    =\frac{1}{2|\alpha|}\frac{dI}{dt},
    \qquad \beta_{\mathrm{phase}}=+\frac12.
    \label{eq:drag-half-derivative}
\end{equation}
The finite-pulse optimum depends on the chosen objective and need not
coincide with the minimum-leakage coefficient.

Figure~\ref{fig:pulse-modification-waveforms} shows the envelope, midpoint
inversion, and DRAG derivative terms, including the polynomial correction
defined in Eq.~\ref{eq:pulse-modification-polynomial}.

We choose $\beta$ by minimizing the root-mean-square shift of the simulated
dip centers from the unperturbed qubit frequency across six peak drives
from 10 to $60~\mathrm{MHz}$. For both hard-cutoff and polynomial-corrected
pulses, $\beta\simeq0.5$ minimizes these shifts
[Fig.~\ref{fig:drag-beta-optimization}].
Without DRAG ($\beta=0$), the simulated RMS offset is about $8~\mathrm{kHz}$.
At $\beta=0.5$, it falls to $400~\mathrm{Hz}$, and to $30~\mathrm{Hz}$
with additional polynomial shaping.
In the experimental comparison, the center variation about each curve's
own mean decreases from approximately 24 to 4 to $2~\mathrm{kHz}$ for
echo, DRAG, and polynomial-corrected DRAG, respectively.
These measurements use fixed $\beta_{\mathrm{acq}}=1.25$ in the same
quadrature convention, rather than an experimental scan of $\beta$.

\subsection{Cutoff smoothing and drive robustness}

For the root-Lorentzian core
\begin{equation}
    f(t)=\left[1+(t/\sigma)^2\right]^{-1/2},
    \qquad \sigma=\frac{L}{2}\frac{c}{\sqrt{1-c^2}},
\end{equation}
hard truncation leaves an endpoint jump of size $c$.
Subtracting this value and applying a polynomial window gives
\begin{equation}
    g_p(t)=\frac{f(t)-c}{1-c}
    \left[1-\left(\frac{2t}{L}\right)^2\right]^p,
    \qquad |t|<L/2,
    \label{eq:pulse-modification-polynomial}
\end{equation}
and zero outside. With $p=2$, the envelope and its first two derivatives
vanish at the cutoff. The parameter $c$ sets the core width, while
$g_p(0)=1$ and the corrected endpoints are zero.
The dynamical comparisons normalize the full complex envelope to the
same peak $\Omega_{\mathrm{pk}}$.

Figure~\ref{fig:edge-smoothing-results} compares hard and smoothed cutoffs
at fixed $\beta=0.5$ and $T=50~\mathrm{ns}$. At
$\Omega_{\mathrm{pk}}/(2\pi)=100~\mathrm{MHz}$, polynomial smoothing
reduces $\mathrm{FWHM}/\Gamma_{T_2^*}$ from about 12.6 to 5.1 and raises the
background-referenced dip contrast $C$ from 0.06 to 0.27.
The improvement persists across the sampled 50--$100~\mathrm{MHz}$ range.
A matched pulse without DRAG has
$\mathrm{FWHM}/\Gamma_{T_2^*}\simeq14.5$:
cutoff shaping therefore supplies most of the additional narrowing,
although the normalized width remains above unity.
