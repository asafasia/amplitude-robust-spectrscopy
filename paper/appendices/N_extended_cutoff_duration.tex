\section{Extended Cutoff and Pulse-Duration Range}
\label{sec:extended-cutoff-duration}

Figure~\ref{fig:extended-cutoff-duration} extends the $L=20~\mu\mathrm{s}$
maps in main-text Fig.~4 to $L=10$, 20, 30, 40, and $50~\mu\mathrm{s}$,
over $10^{-5}\leq c\leq1$ and $1\leq\Omega_0/(2\pi)\leq100~\mathrm{MHz}$.
These three-level simulations use the polynomial-corrected DRAG pulse
of Sec.~\ref{sec:pulse-modifications}, with $p=2$, $\beta=0.5$, and a
$16~\mathrm{ns}$ midpoint transition. Here $\Omega_0$ is the unsigned
midpoint scale, before smoothing and DRAG.

The cutoff $c$ sets the root-Lorentzian core width; the corrected endpoints
vanish for every $c$. Increasing $L$ at fixed $c$ also broadens the core.
At $c=1$, the envelope has the smooth limit
\begin{equation}
    g(t)\big|_{c=1}=
    \left[1-\left(\frac{2t}{L}\right)^2\right]^3.
    \label{eq:extended-cutoff-one-limit}
\end{equation}

We compare the normalized Gaussian-fit width of the central dip
$\mathrm{FWHM}/\Gamma_{T_2^*}$ and background-relative contrast
\begin{equation}
    C=P_{\mathrm{bg}}(\delta_*)-P_e^{\mathrm{fit}}(\delta_*),
    \qquad \delta=\Delta/(2\pi),
    \label{eq:extended-cutoff-contrast}
\end{equation}
where $\delta_*$ is the fitted dip center and $P_{\mathrm{bg}}$ is the local
quadratic background (Sec.~\ref{sec:numerical-model}). Contrast is an excitation-probability
difference, not a signal-to-noise ratio. Unresolved or invalid dips are
excluded; an accepted central-dip fit does not imply a unique resonance.
The third column shows $Q=C\Gamma_{T_2^*}/\mathrm{FWHM}$, as in
Fig.~\ref{fig:duration-resolution-comparison}; red contours outline
regions with $Q>0.10$.

At $L=20~\mu\mathrm{s}$ and approximately $50~\mathrm{MHz}$ drive,
$c=10^{-5}$ gives $\mathrm{FWHM}/\Gamma_{T_2^*}\simeq2.86$ but negligible
contrast ($C=0.0011$). Near $c=10^{-3}$, the width remains comparable
at $3.18$ while contrast rises to $0.40$.
Larger cutoffs broaden the feature and reduce visibility, reaching
a normalized width of $14.9$ and $C=0.14$ at $c=1$ despite the smooth endpoints.

Duration changes this balance. At the same drive and
$c=1.048\times10^{-3}$, increasing $L$ from 10 to $30~\mu\mathrm{s}$
reduces $\mathrm{FWHM}/\Gamma_{T_2^*}$ from 5.57 to 2.36 while contrast falls from
0.644 to 0.223. At 40 and $50~\mu\mathrm{s}$, the widths decrease further to
1.90 and 1.53, while contrast falls to 0.115 and 0.052,
respectively. Narrowing the central feature therefore comes at the cost of
visibility: the useful duration balances resolution against contrast loss from
relaxation and dephasing.

We select one cutoff per duration
by maximizing the minimum $Q$ over the sampled
10.235--$100~\mathrm{MHz}$ drive range, requiring an accepted dip at
every drive. This tests amplitude robustness without retuning the cutoff.

The selected cutoff decreases with duration. Among the five durations,
$20~\mu\mathrm{s}$ gives the best worst-case $Q$, with minimum contrast
$0.296$ and maximum $\mathrm{FWHM}/\Gamma_{T_2^*}\simeq3.50$ over the sampled
drive range. The $30~\mu\mathrm{s}$ pulse lowers the maximum normalized width to
$2.39$ at the cost of minimum contrast $0.187$; longer
pulses lose further visibility. These sampled numerical results identify
a linewidth--contrast compromise; $Q$ is a tradeoff score,
not a frequency-estimation uncertainty.
