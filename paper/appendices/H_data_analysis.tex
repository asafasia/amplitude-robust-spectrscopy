\section{Experimental--Simulation Comparison}
\label{sec:data-analysis}

The amplitude-robustness data set was acquired on qubit q1 using active reset
and state-discriminated readout.  Each point contains 40 repetitions.  The
detuning axis spans $-0.5$ to $+0.5~\mathrm{MHz}$ in $1~\mathrm{kHz}$ steps,
and the data comprise 100 fixed-amplitude slices.  The pulse record contains a
root--Lorentzian edge-to-peak ratio $c=0.005$, a pulse length
$L=10~\mu\mathrm{s}$, and an unscaled peak output amplitude of $0.2$.  The
peak Rabi frequency is reconstructed from the saved q1 square-pulse
calibration, including this peak output amplitude.

For the matched comparisons in
Figs.~\ref{fig:amplitude-robustness-2d} and
\ref{fig:amplitude-robustness-linecuts},
we integrate the two-level Lindblad model of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:numerical-master-equation} on the complete experimental
detuning and calibrated peak-Rabi grids.  The calculation uses the recorded
echo--root--Lorentzian pulse parameters $L=10~\mu\mathrm{s}$ and $c=0.005$
together with the measurement-linked values
$T_1=\MeasuredTOne~\mu\mathrm{s}$ and
$T_\phi=\EffectiveTPhi~\mu\mathrm{s}$.  No affine readout calibration or
fitted frequency offset is applied.

To quantify the complete sweep without an additional figure, each
fixed-amplitude trace is analyzed independently.  After discarding 40 points
from each frequency edge, we apply a one-sample Gaussian filter and fit the
operational dip estimator
\begin{equation}
    y(\delta)
    = y_0-A\exp\!\left[-\frac{(\delta-\delta_0)^2}{2\sigma_f^2}\right],
    \label{eq:dip-gaussian-estimator}
\end{equation}
where $\delta=\Delta/(2\pi)$ is cyclic detuning, $A$ is the contrast,
$\delta_0$ is the fitted center, and
\begin{equation}
    \Gamma_f
    =2\sqrt{2\ln2}\,|\sigma_f|.
\end{equation}
The Gaussian provides a consistent linewidth estimator and is not intended as
an exact model of the complete response.  We normalize its fitted linewidth to
the measurement-linked coherence reference
\begin{equation}
    \Gamma_{f,T_2}
    =\frac{1}{\pi T_{2,\mathrm{eff}}}
    =\EffectiveCoherenceFwhm~\mathrm{kHz},
    \qquad T_{2,\mathrm{eff}}=\EffectiveTTwo~\mu\mathrm{s}.
\end{equation}
The corresponding dimensionless linewidth, normalized resolution, and
contrast-weighted resolution are
\begin{equation}
    W=\frac{\Gamma_f}{\Gamma_{f,T_2}},
    \qquad
    R=\frac{\Gamma_{f,T_2}}{\Gamma_f},
    \qquad
    Q=A R.
    \label{eq:normalized-resolution-metrics}
\end{equation}
Here $W=1$ and $R=1$ denote the coherence-reference linewidth and reciprocal
resolution, while $Q$ penalizes narrow features with small contrast.  The
experimental and simulated traces are processed with the same fit and quality
gates: finite fit covariance, $A\geq0.02$,
$\Gamma_f\le0.12~\mathrm{MHz}$, and
$|\delta_0|\le0.050~\mathrm{MHz}$.  Of 100 amplitude slices, 62 experimental
and 54 simulated slices pass.  Failed slices remain missing rather than being
reported as zero.  We use $Q\geq0.10$ only as an operational
signal--resolution threshold.

\subsection{Constant-pulse reference window}

For comparison, we use the analytic steady-state Torrey constant-drive tool
employed by the IPS FWHM plots.  With saturation parameter
$s=\Omega_0^2 T_1T_2$, the power-broadened linewidth and population contrast
are
\begin{equation}
    W \equiv \frac{\Gamma_f}{\Gamma_{f,T_2}}
      = \sqrt{1+s},
    \qquad
    A = \frac{s}{2(1+s)}.
\end{equation}
Here $T_1=\MeasuredTOne~\mu\mathrm{s}$ and
$T_2=\EffectiveTTwo~\mu\mathrm{s}$, consistent with the normalization used
above.  Increasing a constant drive raises the contrast but power broadens the
line and lowers $R=1/W$.  In the low-drive limit the linewidth approaches
$\Gamma_{f,T_2}=1/(\pi T_2)=43.5~\mathrm{kHz}$.  The operational condition
$Q=AR\geq0.10$ holds over
$\Omega_0/2\pi=0.0045$--$0.0385~\mathrm{MHz}$, with an analytic maximum
$Q=0.192$ at $0.0116~\mathrm{MHz}$.  This threshold summarizes the
signal--resolution trade-off and is not a universal physical boundary.
