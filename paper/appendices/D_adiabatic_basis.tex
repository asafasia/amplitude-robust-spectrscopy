\section{Adiabatic-Basis Description}
\label{sec:adiabatic-basis}

For one half of the pulse, the rotating-frame Hamiltonian can be written
\begin{equation}
    \frac{H(t)}{\hbar}
    =\frac{1}{2}
    \begin{bmatrix}
        -\Delta & \Omega(t)\\
        \Omega(t) & \Delta
    \end{bmatrix}.
    \label{eq:rotated-hamiltonian-matrix}
\end{equation}
Define
\begin{equation}
    \epsilon(t)=\sqrt{\Delta^2+\Omega^2(t)},
    \qquad
    \vartheta(t)=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{atan2}[\Omega(t),\Delta].
\end{equation}
Both $\epsilon$ and $\dot{\vartheta}$ have units of inverse seconds, whereas
$\vartheta$ is dimensionless.  The instantaneous eigenvalues are
$E_-=-\hbar\epsilon/2$ and $E_+=+\hbar\epsilon/2$.  Taking the instantaneous eigenvectors as the
columns of
\begin{equation}
    R(t)=
    \begin{bmatrix}
        \cos\vartheta & \sin\vartheta\\
        -\sin\vartheta & \cos\vartheta
    \end{bmatrix},
\end{equation}
the transformed Hamiltonian is
$H_a=R^{\mathsf T}HR-i\hbar R^{\mathsf T}\dot R$.  Therefore
\begin{equation}
    \frac{H_a(t)}{\hbar}
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        -\epsilon/2 & -i\dot{\vartheta}\\
        i\dot{\vartheta} & \epsilon/2
    \end{bmatrix},
    \qquad
    \dot{\vartheta}
    =\frac{\Delta\dot{\Omega}}
    {2(\Delta^2+\Omega^2)}.
    \label{eq:adiabatic-hamiltonian}
\end{equation}
The nonadiabatic coupling is $|\dot{\vartheta}|$, while the instantaneous
angular-frequency gap is $\epsilon$.  Adiabatic following therefore
requires~\cite{boradjiev_power_2013}
\begin{equation}
    |\dot{\vartheta}|\ll\epsilon.
    \label{eq:adiabatic-condition}
\end{equation}
Replacing the inequality by equality provides the local crossover estimate
\begin{equation}
    \sqrt{\Delta_b^2+\Omega^2(t_0)}
    =
    \frac{|\Delta_b\dot{\Omega}(t_0)|}
    {2[\Delta_b^2+\Omega^2(t_0)]},
    \label{eq:adiabatic-boundary}
\end{equation}
where $t_0$ is the time at which the nonadiabatic coupling is largest for the
detuning under consideration.  This is a scaling criterion, not an exact
linewidth formula.

This smooth-basis construction applies separately to each half of the echo
pulse; the imposed phase jump at $t=0$ is a control discontinuity and is not
represented by $\dot\vartheta$ in this local adiabatic estimate.

For $L^{(n)}(t)\sim|t|^{-2n}$, the inverse-power-tail derivation of
Boradjiev and Vitanov requires $2n>1$.  For an untruncated generalized
Lorentzian with $n>1/2$, it gives
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_b\tau
    \propto
    (\Omega_0\tau)^{-1/(2n-1)}.
    \label{eq:generalized-lorentzian-scaling}
\end{equation}
As $n\to1/2^+$, the exponent $1/(2n-1)$ diverges, indicating the strongest
power-narrowing trend within the asymptotic family.  The exact
root-Lorentzian case $n=1/2$ is nevertheless marginal and lies outside the
domain of Eq.~\eqref{eq:generalized-lorentzian-scaling}; substituting
$n=1/2$ into that power law is invalid.  In particular, the singularity does
not imply zero FWHM.  The finite pulse length, nonzero endpoint amplitude,
decoherence, and echo phase inversion set the observed $\Gamma_f$, which is
evaluated from the complete simulated and experimental response in this work.
