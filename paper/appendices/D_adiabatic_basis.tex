\section{Adiabatic-Basis Description}
\label{sec:adiabatic-basis}

For one half of the pulse, the rotating-frame Hamiltonian can be written
\begin{equation}
    \frac{H(t)}{\hbar}
    =\frac{1}{2}
    \begin{bmatrix}
        -\Delta & \Omega(t)\\
        \Omega(t) & \Delta
    \end{bmatrix}.
    \label{eq:rotated-hamiltonian-matrix}
\end{equation}
Define
\begin{equation}
    \epsilon(t)=\sqrt{\Delta^2+\Omega^2(t)},
    \qquad
    \vartheta(t)=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{atan2}[\Omega(t),\Delta].
\end{equation}
The instantaneous eigenvalues are $\pm\hbar\epsilon/2$.  In the corresponding
adiabatic basis, with phases chosen consistently across the pulse,
\begin{equation}
    \frac{H_a(t)}{\hbar}
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        -\epsilon/2 & -i\dot{\vartheta}\\
        i\dot{\vartheta} & \epsilon/2
    \end{bmatrix},
    \qquad
    \dot{\vartheta}
    =\frac{\Delta\dot{\Omega}}
    {2(\Delta^2+\Omega^2)}.
    \label{eq:adiabatic-hamiltonian}
\end{equation}
Adiabatic following requires~\cite{boradjiev_power_2013}
\begin{equation}
    2|\dot{\vartheta}|\ll\epsilon.
    \label{eq:adiabatic-condition}
\end{equation}
Replacing the inequality by equality provides the local crossover estimate
\begin{equation}
    \sqrt{\Delta_b^2+\Omega^2(t_0)}
    =
    \frac{|\Delta_b\dot{\Omega}(t_0)|}
    {\Delta_b^2+\Omega^2(t_0)},
    \label{eq:adiabatic-boundary}
\end{equation}
where $t_0$ is the time at which the nonadiabatic coupling is largest for the
detuning under consideration.  This is a scaling criterion, not an exact
linewidth formula.

For an untruncated generalized Lorentzian tail with exponent $n>1/2$, the
usual adiabatic estimate gives
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_b\tau
    \propto
    (\Omega_0\tau)^{-1/(2n-1)}.
    \label{eq:generalized-lorentzian-scaling}
\end{equation}
The root-Lorentzian case $n=1/2$ is marginal and lies outside the domain of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:generalized-lorentzian-scaling}; substituting $n=1/2$ into that
power law is therefore invalid.  The linewidth of the finite, echo-modulated
root-Lorentzian pulse is evaluated numerically and experimentally in this
work.
