\section{Three-Level AC-Stark Shift of the \texorpdfstring{$f_{01}$}{f01} Resonance}
\label{sec:ac-stark-shift}

We summarize the weak-drive AC-Stark scale of the $f_{01}$ transition
using the three-level rotating-wave model. The detuning convention is
$\Delta=\omega_d-\omega_{01}$. The basis
$\{|g\rangle,|e\rangle,|f\rangle\}$ retains $|f\rangle$ as a dynamical
state; in the weak-drive interpretation, virtual
$|e\rangle\leftrightarrow|f\rangle$ coupling produces the leading
shift of $f_{01}$. In this basis, the rotating-frame
Hamiltonian of Eq.~\eqref{eq:numerical-hamiltonian} is
\begin{equation}
    \frac{H(t)}{\hbar}=
    \begin{pmatrix}
        0 & \Omega(t)/2 & 0 \\
        \Omega(t)/2 & -\Delta & \Omega(t)/\sqrt{2} \\
        0 & \Omega(t)/\sqrt{2} & -2\Delta+\alpha
    \end{pmatrix},
    \label{eq:qutrit-ac-stark-hamiltonian}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha=\omega_{12}-\omega_{01}<0$.  For the constant-drive reference, virtual
coupling between $|e\rangle$ and $|f\rangle$ gives the weak-drive result
for a weakly anharmonic transmon~\cite{PhysRevA.76.042319,schneider_multilevel_2018}:
\begin{equation}
    \delta f_{01}^{(\mathrm{const})}
    \equiv \frac{\Delta_{\mathrm{res}}}{2\pi}
    \simeq
    -\frac{(\Omega_0/2\pi)^2}
    {2(\alpha/2\pi)}.
    \label{eq:qutrit-ac-stark-shift}
\end{equation}
Because $\alpha<0$, the dressed $f_{01}$ resonance of the constant-drive reference
moves to positive detuning.

For the shaped pulses, $\Omega(t)=\Omega_0 f(t)$ with the root-Lorentzian
envelope of Eq.~\eqref{eq:finite-generalized-lorentzian}, order $n=1/2$, and
endpoint ratio $c$. A useful weak-drive phase-average estimate is
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    \delta f_{01}^{(\mathrm{shape})}
    &\simeq
    -\frac{(\Omega_0/2\pi)^2}{2(\alpha/2\pi)}
    \left\langle f^2\right\rangle_t,
    \\
    \left\langle f^2\right\rangle_t
    &=\frac{2\sigma}{L}\tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{L}{2\sigma}\right),
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:shaped-ac-stark-average}
\end{equation}
where
$\sigma=(L/2)/\sqrt{c^{-2}-1}$. The echo phase inversion changes
$f(t)\rightarrow-f(t)$ in the second half and therefore does not reverse this
quadratic Stark term.  Consequently, the root-Lorentzian and
echo-root-Lorentzian pulses have the same leading phase-average estimate.

The position of a peak or dip in the final population response need not
coincide with this phase-average estimate: it also depends on finite-pulse
interference and dissipation. Section~\ref{sec:pulse-modifications}
therefore evaluates the spectral center directly from the simulated
response when comparing DRAG coefficients. The rotating-wave model omits
the counter-rotating Bloch--Siegert contribution~\cite{tuorila_stark_2010}.

\subsection{AC-Stark scale at a common peak drive}

To compare the shift of the constant-drive reference with the shaped-pulse
phase-average estimate, take a common nominal peak drive
$\Omega_0/(2\pi)=50~\mathrm{MHz}$. For the constant-drive reference,
the weak-drive estimate from Eq.~\eqref{eq:qutrit-ac-stark-shift} is approximately
$5.8~\mathrm{MHz}$.  For the $20~\mu\mathrm{s}$ root-Lorentzian envelope
with $c=0.001$, multiplication by
$\langle f^2\rangle_t=0.001570$ reduces the phase-average estimate to
$9.1~\mathrm{kHz}$. These estimates compare the two envelopes at the same
nominal peak drive using Eqs.~\eqref{eq:qutrit-ac-stark-shift}
and~\eqref{eq:shaped-ac-stark-average}. The echo phase
inversion does not cancel the quadratic Stark term; rather, the small
time-averaged squared envelope suppresses it, while finite-pulse interference
determines the experimentally visible central minimum.
