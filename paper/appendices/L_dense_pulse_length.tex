\section{Dense Pulse-Length Dependence}
\label{sec:dense-pulse-length}

Measurements on the second qubit vary the pulse duration from
$L=1$ to $40~\mu\mathrm{s}$ at fixed cutoff $c=0.005$.
The echo has an abrupt midpoint phase jump, without DRAG or AC-Stark
compensation. Increasing $L$ stretches the entire envelope, including its
core. Linewidths are extracted from the background-subtracted half-height
crossings, retaining only features resolved above the measurement noise.

Longer pulses produce narrower features but progressively lose measurable
contrast. At $L=25~\mu\mathrm{s}$, only 32 of 400 experimental traces yield
accepted widths; none do so at $L=30$, 35, or $40~\mu\mathrm{s}$.
This loss of accepted widths indicates that the spectral feature is no
longer reliably resolved, rather than that its linewidth vanishes.
The long-duration results therefore represent only a small surviving
subset of drive amplitudes.

A matched three-level calculation generally predicts broader features than
the experiment at long durations. Comparing accepted widths at the same
drive and duration, the RMS difference increases from
$34.2~\mathrm{kHz}$ at $L=2~\mu\mathrm{s}$ to $302.5~\mathrm{kHz}$ at
$L=20~\mu\mathrm{s}$. These results reinforce the resolution--contrast
tradeoff in Fig.~\ref{fig:duration-resolution-comparison}: the narrowest
line is not necessarily the most useful operating point.
The changing accepted subset and unresolved contribution of drift prevent
identification of a unique optimal duration or a universal
coherence-limited linewidth plateau.
