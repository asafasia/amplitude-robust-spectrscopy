Figure~\ref{fig:duration-resolution-comparison} compares pulse durations at
fixed $c=0.005$. The left column shows the constant-drive Bloch reference
over 1--$100~\mathrm{kHz}$, while the right shows echo pulses over
0.1--$20~\mathrm{MHz}$. Both amplitude axes are logarithmic, and the
vertical scale is shared within each row.
Smaller FWHM means finer spectral resolution. In the echo column, longer
pulses give narrower weak-drive features in row (a), but lower contrast in row (b);
the narrowest features can therefore be difficult to detect.
Longer evolution also increases exposure to energy relaxation on the scale
$T_1$: the echo does not reverse population decay, which, together with
dephasing, limits the useful contrast.

Row (c) combines both quantities as
$Q=C\Gamma_{T_2^*}/\mathrm{FWHM}$, where $C$ is the fitted contrast.
For $L=5$--$20~\mu\mathrm{s}$, the illustrative $Q=0.10$ guide highlights a
useful linewidth--contrast balance over MHz-wide echo-drive intervals,
compared with the kilohertz-scale interval of the constant-drive reference.
These spans should be compared using the axis values, since the columns
use different amplitude ranges and units. Increasing duration trades resolution
against visibility, rather than improving both without limit.

Sections~\ref{sec:pulse-modifications}--\ref{sec:extended-cutoff-duration}
examine DRAG, cutoff-edge shaping, and smoothing of the midpoint phase jump
to further improve resonance stability and linewidth--contrast performance
over a wider drive range.
